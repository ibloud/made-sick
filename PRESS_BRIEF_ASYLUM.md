# Press brief: *Asylum*, independent success, and Made Sick

**Prepared:** September 6, 2026  
**Status:** Background material for journalists; not a press release from Ren, Chris Webby, Inpatient, Sick Boi, or their representatives.

## The verified news hook

*Asylum*, credited by the Official Charts Company to REN/INPATIENT/CHRIS WEBBY, reached:

- No. 1 on the UK Official Independent Album Breakers Chart for one week;
- No. 3 on the UK Official Album Downloads Chart;
- No. 9 on the UK Official Independent Albums Chart; and
- No. 38 on the UK Official Albums Sales Chart.

The chart entries are dated July 30, 2026. These are distinct chart categories. They do **not** establish that the album was No. 1 on every chart or in every country.

High Times published Javier Hasse's long-form review of the album on September 3, 2026. Hasse described the record as a 15-song collaboration created after Ren and Chris Webby met through an online exchange and worked together in Connecticut.

## What Chris Webby said

In a public-facing social-media video supplied to this project, Webby thanked Hasse and High Times for the review. He characterized the response from the wider music industry as “crickets” and said, “we made our own industry out here.”

Those statements express Webby's perspective. They are not, by themselves, evidence of coordinated exclusion, suppression, or a complete absence of other coverage.

**Publication check required:** obtain the original post URL, publication date, and permission or a direct comment from Webby or his representative before relying on the supplied copy of the video as a primary source.

## Why it may matter beyond one album

A responsible follow-up story could examine how independent artists convert direct audience relationships into measurable chart performance, how specialist publications identify work overlooked by general music desks, and what kinds of distribution or label services independent releases still use.

Questions for reporting include:

1. Who financed, owns, and distributes the recording?
2. Which territories and storefronts produced the reported results?
3. What marketing, playlist, touring, merchandise, or fan-community activity supported the release?
4. How do the artists define “independent” in this project?
5. Which music publications were approached, and did any decline or not respond?
6. What support or boundaries do the artists want when listeners connect their work to illness, disability, neurodivergence, substance use, or mental health?

## Made Sick connection

Made Sick is a consent-first proposal for recognizing creators who make meaningful work through illness, disability, adversity, or major life goals. It proposes creator-controlled profiles, source labels, clear boundaries around health information, and links to qualified support resources.

The *Asylum* story is relevant as a **publicly documented example for discussion**, particularly where creative work, medical treatment, neurodivergence, mental-health themes, independent production, and community intersect.

### Required disclosure

Made Sick is an independent Loptr Lab prototype and commentary project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Ren, Chris Webby, Inpatient, Sick Boi, High Times, the Official Charts Company, or Reuters. Ren and Chris Webby are not enrolled profiles unless they affirmatively opt in. Made Sick does not diagnose creators, authenticate every personal statement, or turn lyrics into medical evidence.

## Editorial safeguards

- Attribute health and identity statements to the person and original source.
- Do not infer a diagnosis from lyrics, imagery, collaborators, relatives, or fan interpretation.
- Distinguish chart categories from overall national album rankings.
- Describe “industry silence” as Webby's characterization unless reporting independently establishes its scope.
- Give the artists, their representatives, distributors, and any criticized organization a fair opportunity to comment.
- Link qualified help where coverage discusses crisis, trauma, substance use, or medical treatment.
- Clearly label fan, campaign, and unaffiliated commentary.
